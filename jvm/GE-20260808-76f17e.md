### Symptom
`@QuarkusTest` fails with `UnsatisfiedResolutionException` when injecting a `@Scheduled` bean that is listed in `quarkus.arc.exclude-types` in test `application.properties`.

### Context
Scheduled jobs (e.g. `TrialSafetyAggregationJob`, `SiteEnrollmentTrajectoryJob`) are excluded from tests via `quarkus.arc.exclude-types` to prevent the scheduler from firing during test execution. This is correct for tests that don't need the bean. But when an integration test needs to call the job's methods directly (e.g. `aggregateTrial()`), the bean doesn't exist in the CDI container.

### What was tried (didn't work)
- Injecting the bean while it's in the exclude list — `UnsatisfiedResolutionException`
- Removing from exclude list without setting a long interval — risk of scheduler firing during slow CI runs

### Root cause
`quarkus.arc.exclude-types` completely removes the bean from CDI — it's not instantiated at all. There's no way to have the bean available but its `@Scheduled` method disabled.

### Fix
Two changes required:

1. Remove the bean from `quarkus.arc.exclude-types` in test `application.properties`
2. Set the scheduler interval to a very long value to prevent firing:

```properties
# Re-enable for integration tests that inject the real bean
# (removed from quarkus.arc.exclude-types)
casehub.clinical.trial-safety.interval=9999h
```

The 9999h interval ensures the scheduler never fires during test execution while the bean remains injectable.

### Why this is non-obvious
The exclude-types pattern is well-established for preventing scheduler noise in tests. The gotcha is that it's all-or-nothing — you can't selectively disable just the `@Scheduled` annotation while keeping the bean available for direct method calls. The long-interval workaround is the only option short of refactoring the job into a separate scheduling bean and a logic bean.
