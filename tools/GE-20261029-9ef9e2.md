## Symptom

Adding more context to a multi-section LLM prompt causes output quality to shift register (e.g., from somatic/experiential to cognitive/analytical) while overall prose quality holds steady. The obvious diagnosis — "too much context" or "attention limits" — leads to the wrong fix (removing sections).

## Context

Character prompts with 14+ sections including a Barrett somatic instruction ("feel, don't think") and separate cognitive-analytical sections about relationships ("Prey. Trust 0.1. Predictable."). Register dropped from 3.74 to 3.13 when relational context was added as separate sections, but quality held at ~3.84.

## Root cause — three layers

**Layer 1 — Redundancy:** The same relational information appears in multiple sections (Social Awareness, Theory of Mind, relational schemas, trust scores). Pure waste that inflates the prompt.

**Layer 2 — Instruction competition:** Cognitive-analytical sections offer the LLM an easier register than the demanded style. LLMs naturally describe internal states analytically. Given both somatic ("feel this in your body") and cognitive ("Trust 0.1, target is predictable") framings, the model gravitates to the path of least resistance. This is why register drops while quality holds — the model produces good prose, just in the wrong register.

**Layer 3 — Phenomenological category error:** Information that is constitutive of a concept is rendered as a separate dimension. A social drive IS its target — you gloat AT someone. Separating drive from target into independent sections forces the LLM to perform cross-reference inference under prompt pressure. That bridge fails under load from many sections.

## Fix

Do not remove sections that represent genuine abilities or context. Instead:

1. **Wire the data flow** — ensure the information the style instruction needs is present AT the point where the style applies (e.g., targeted somatic descriptions that reference who they're about, not separate analytical sections)
2. **Eliminate cognitive escape routes** — when both somatic and analytical framings are available for the same information, the analytical one wins. Fold the information into the style-consistent rendering.
3. **Make cross-references explicit** — if concept A needs concept B, compose them together rather than placing them in separate sections and hoping the LLM bridges them.

### Why this is non-obvious

The natural response to register degradation is removing context ("the model is overwhelmed"). But removing cognitive sections removes abilities — the character can no longer reason about relationships. The actual fix is rewiring how information flows through the prompt: correct appraisal (drives know their targets) and targeted prose (somatic descriptions reference who they're about), not less information.

### Alternatives considered

- **Remove competing sections** — lobotomizes the character; removes cognitive abilities to fix a rendering problem
- **Shorten all sections** — reduces the symptom without addressing the architectural cause; register partially recovers but information is lost
- **Reorder sections** (put style instruction last) — marginal improvement; doesn't address instruction competition or the cross-reference problem
