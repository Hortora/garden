### Symptom

When designing multi-agent LLM characters with distinct voices, the natural
approach is to prescribe specific catchphrases in the character definition:

```yaml
signature-phrases:
  - "Oh my goodness!"
  - "Well I do declare!"
  - "Bless your heart!"
```

This produces consistent but limited voice — the character repeats the prescribed
phrases but doesn't generate richer regional expression.

### Context

Multi-agent scenario with 5 characters, each with a distinct regional voice
archetype. Tested with 3 ablation runs (~300 events each):
1. Wacky Races characters (model has prior knowledge)
2. Generic characters with prescribed catchphrases (no prior knowledge)
3. Generic characters with style-directive only (no prior knowledge, no phrases)

### Fix

Replace prescribed catchphrases with a style-directive that tells the model
to draw from its cultural knowledge:

```yaml
voice:
  accent: East London working class
  register: gruff collective voice
  style-directive: >-
    Lean heavily into East London colloquialisms, Cockney rhyming slang,
    and working-class London expressions. Drop h's, use local idioms,
    and speak like genuine East Enders would.
```

### Results

Style-directive produced richer output for 4 of 5 character archetypes:

| Character | Prescribed | Style-directive | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Southern belle | "butter my biscuits!" | "HUSH my mouth" (deeper Southernism) | Equal |
| East London mob | "Blimey, lads!" | "legged it", "like headless chickens" | Style-directive |
| Upper-class English | "SPLENDID!" | "Berkshire Treasure Hunting Society" | Style-directive |
| Earnest gentleman | "By Jove!" | Added physical comedy stage directions | Style-directive |
| Theatrical villain | "Tsk tsk TSK!" | No spontaneous evil laugh | Prescribed |

### Why this is non-obvious

The instinct is to prescribe specific phrases for consistency. But LLMs have
deep cultural knowledge of regional stereotypes — deeper than any prescribed
list. The style-directive unlocks that knowledge instead of constraining it.

The design principle: **prescribe structure, not content — tell the model
where to fish, not which fish to catch.**

The exception proves the rule: the theatrical villain archetype is not
regionally grounded, so the model has less cultural material to draw from.
For non-regional archetypes, keep 1-2 prescribed anchors (especially
distinctive laughs or verbal tics).
