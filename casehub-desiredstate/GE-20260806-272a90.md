### Context
The deployment module in casehub-ops manages CaseHub topology via desired-state reconciliation. Adding a new node type (e.g. DetectionNodeSpec as the 6th type) requires touching a specific set of files.

### The 6-component pattern
1. **Spec record** in `ops-api/deployment/` implementing sealed `DeploymentNodeSpec`
2. **GoalCompiler** `compileEntries()` call in `DeploymentGoalCompiler`
3. **Provision handler** in `deployment/handler/` (provision + deprovision)
4. **Drift checker** in `deployment/drift/` implementing `NodeDriftChecker`
5. **Provisioner switch arms** — `doProvision()` and `doDeprovision()` in `DeploymentNodeProvisioner`
6. **Adapter `handledTypes()`** — `DeploymentActualStateAdapter`

### The 4 ripple updates
- `DeploymentNodeSpec` sealed permits list
- `PlanStoreMapper` — TWO mixins: `NodeSpecMixin` AND `DeploymentNodeSpecMixin`
- `DeploymentApprovalEvaluator` — risk classification AND summary generation switches
- ALL positional `DeploymentGoals` constructor call sites (every test that constructs goals)

### Why this is non-obvious
The 6 core components follow the existing pattern and are discoverable. The 4 ripple updates are not — especially the two PlanStoreMapper mixins (easy to miss one) and the DeploymentGoals positional-arg cascade across every test file. The DeploymentGoals ripple was the most time-consuming part of adding DetectionNodeSpec.

### Alternative
Could use a builder pattern for DeploymentGoals to avoid the positional-arg cascade, but the record pattern is deliberate — it makes missing fields a compile error rather than a runtime default.
